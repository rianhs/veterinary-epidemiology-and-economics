The level of analysis is the level at which a problem is studied. It can be a single unit, a group of units, or the system around them. In veterinary epidemiology, the common levels are the individual animal, the animal population, and the animal product and economic system.

## Background

The same disease looks different from each level. A sick hen shows signs, a flock shows a death rate, and an egg chain shows eggs that reach buyers. Each view raises its own question, needs its own data, and leads to its own decision.

A problem that is studied at the wrong level gives an answer to the wrong question. A veterinarian who looks only at the sick hen can treat that hen and will not learn how far the disease has spread. A veterinarian who looks only at the flock will not learn where the eggs went. The level of analysis names the level at which the question is asked, so the data and the decision fit the question.

[Epidemiology](epidemiology.md) works mainly at the population level. The other two levels still matter. Individual animals produce the patterns of a population, and the economic system shapes both the spread and the consequences of disease.

## Characteristics

The table shows the three levels, with the kind of question and the role of each level in the course.

| Level | Examples of what is studied | Example question | Role |
|-|-|-|-|
| Individual animal | Clinical disease, infection, welfare state, productivity | What is wrong with this animal? | Source of population patterns |
| Animal population | Herd, flock, kennel, shelter, village population, wildlife group | How many animals are affected, and where? | Main level of analysis |
| Animal product and economic system | Milk, meat, eggs, hides, feed, veterinary medicines, veterinary services, markets, regulations | Where do the products go, and what does the disease cost? | Data source, pathway for introduction, place where economic consequences appear |

### Individual animal

At this level, the object of study is one animal. Typical findings are clinical signs, a test result, a body condition score, or the milk yield of one cow. The decision is about that animal, for example whether to treat it.

### Animal population

At this level, the object of study is a group of animals that share a place or a production system. Typical findings are a prevalence, a mortality rate, or a map of cases by village. The decision is about the group, for example whether to vaccinate the whole flock. This is the main level of the course.

### Animal product and economic system

At this level, the object of study is the system that surrounds the animals. It includes animal products such as milk, meat, and eggs. It also includes feed, veterinary medicines, veterinary services, markets, and regulations.

The system level has three roles. It is a source of data, for example sales records or inspection records. It is a pathway through which a disease or a hazard can enter a population, for example through feed or traded animals. It is also the place where economic consequences appear, such as lost sales or price changes.

### The same disease at two levels

A clinical question and a population question can concern the same disease. For a case of Salmonella in a hen, the clinical question is how to treat the hen. For the flock, the population question is how many hens carry the infection and whether the number is rising. The answers come from different data, and one answer does not replace the other.

### Level of analysis and unit of analysis

The two terms are related and have different meanings. The level of analysis describes where the problem sits. The [unit of analysis](../glossary.md) is the unit used in a calculation, for example a hen or a pen. A problem at the population level can use animals, pens, or farms as units of analysis.

## Application example

A technical service veterinarian two years after graduation works for a dairy cooperative with 40 member farms. The cooperative reports that its milk is downgraded in price because quality tests fall below the buyer's limit. The numbers in this example are synthetic and are for teaching.

She first looks at the system level. The sales records show that downgraded milk comes from 9 of the 40 farms. She then moves to the population level and finds that, on these 9 farms, 30 of 100 cows have udder inflammation. Finally, she examines individual cows on one farm, to see which cows are affected and which bacteria are present.

Each level answers a different question. The system level shows the size of the economic loss. The population level shows how widespread the disease is, and the individual level shows what is wrong with the affected cows. A plan for the cooperative needs all three, and she can explain to the board which level each number comes from.

## Assumptions and limitations

The three levels are not separate boxes. Most problems span more than one level, and the veterinarian often moves from one level to another during a single investigation.

A result at one level does not automatically hold at another. A high death rate in a flock does not tell which hens are at risk. A treatment that works for one animal does not show that the flock will recover. A veterinarian should state the level of every conclusion. [citation needed]

The level of analysis is a way to organize a problem and does not give a method. After the level is chosen, the veterinarian still needs to define the population, the units, and the data.

## Issues and debates

Other sources describe levels in other ways. Some texts use a hierarchy such as animal, herd, region, and country. Statistical texts use the term multilevel for models that handle groups within groups (Thrusfield & Christley, 2018). [verify] The three levels in this article are a working choice of this course.
