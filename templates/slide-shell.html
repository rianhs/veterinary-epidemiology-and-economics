The case so far

A technical service consultant (GP3.1) visits a layer farm after a rise in mortality.

The consultant sends samples to the laboratory and waits for the test results.

A test finds 12% positive cattle. What is the true prevalence?

The test has a sensitivity of 0.80 and a specificity of 0.95. Choose your prediction.

A positive result and an infection are two different things

The infected animals form one group.

The animals with a positive test form another group. The two groups only partly overlap.

Animals outside the overlap are test errors. They are false positives or false negatives.

A positive result is not always a true case

The apparent prevalence counts all positive results. It includes false positives. It misses the false negatives.

Apparent prevalence is 12%. Se is 0.80 and Sp is 0.95. What is the true prevalence?

Key points

  • The apparent prevalence counts positive results. The true prevalence counts infected animals.
  • False positives push the apparent prevalence up. False negatives pull it down.
  • With known Se and Sp, you can estimate the true prevalence.
  • The correction fails when the apparent prevalence is below 1 − Sp.
