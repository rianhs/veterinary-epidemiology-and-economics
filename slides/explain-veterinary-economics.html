C1 Epidemiological and economic thinking

Explain veterinary economics

A farm has a limited budget and three useful measures. How should the owner choose?

  • S1.2Explain what veterinary economics is

The case: a layer farm in South Sulawesi

The farm loses 120 of its 8,000 hens in one week, mostly in two pens next to the water inlet. The laboratory later finds Salmonella in the hens, and eggs from the same pens have already reached buyers.

Your role: technical consultant (GP3.1), two years after graduation. The owner asks what the problem costs and what to do with a limited budget.

Ask a student to choose A, then B, then C. Each time, ask which alternative the farm gives up and what that is worth. Then ask why the cost of the measure is the same in all three cases and the opportunity cost is not.

Disease takes value away, and control protects it

The analysis must say which values it counts. The case touches five of them.

ProductionMeat, milk, or eggsDead hens and fewer eggs from the two pens

Who receives the benefit of control?

Ask: does the benefit go to the owner who pays, or to people who do not take part in the decision?

Ask the class to vote before you reveal each answer. Link statement 3 to the word externality. Ask who should pay for a measure whose benefit goes mostly to other people.

A measure can work and still cost more than it saves. The decision maker needs both answers.

The owner uses one free day of her own labour to repair the water line. What is the opportunity cost?

A new case: a dairy cooperative

A cooperative of dairy farmers can pay for one measure this year. The options are milking hygiene to lower mastitis, or testing and removal of cattle with brucellosis. This scenario is synthetic and is for teaching.

Which benefit of brucellosis control is a public benefit?

Key points

  • Veterinary economics applies economic principles to animal health, to decide how to use scarce resources.
  • The opportunity cost of a choice is the value of the best alternative that the choice gives up.
  • Disease takes away production, product quality, trade, welfare, and public health, and control protects these values.
  • Some benefits of control go to the owner and others go to other people. The person who pays is not always the person who gains.
  • Epidemiology estimates what happens to disease, and economics estimates what it is worth and what it costs.
