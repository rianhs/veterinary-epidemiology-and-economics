The case: Salmonella on a layer farm

In one week, 120 of the 8,000 hens on a layer farm die. Most of the dead hens come from pens A1 and B1, next to the water inlet.

The laboratory later finds Salmonella in the hens. Eggs from these pens have already reached buyers.

Veterinary economics

Veterinary economics is the use of economic principles to decide how to use scarce resources to protect animal health and welfare.

Read the definition aloud. The next slides explain scarce resources and the choice that follows from them.

Resources are scarce, so someone must choose

The budget pays for one measure. The three measures together cost three times the budget. This condition is called scarcity, and it forces a choice.

Point to the dashed line: the budget ends here. Ask what the owner can do about the other two measures. Possible answers: wait for next month, borrow money, find a cheaper way. Each answer is also a choice.

Every choice means letting go of the best alternative

  • Cost of an action: the amount of resources paid by an owner for that action.
  • Opportunity cost of a choice: the value of the best alternative that the owner gives up.

Example: The farm owner spends an entire day cleaning pens and no money is spent for that day. The day has another use: the owner could work at an egg packing station and earn money. This lost income is the opportunity cost of the day.

Ask: if no money changes hands, is the day free? The day has no cash cost. It still has an opportunity cost, because the owner cannot use the same day twice.

Disease takes value away, and control protects it

The economic analysis must specify which values are taken into account. The case encompasses five values: production, animal welfare, product quality, trade, and public health.

Ask students to find the value in the case for each label. Open the details to see the answers. Ask which value is hardest to give a price.

A benefit can go to the owner or to other people

  • Private benefit goes to the owner who decides and pays. Example: the farm keeps selling eggs.
  • Public benefit goes to other people who do not take part in the decision. Example: buyers avoid illness from eggs that carry Salmonella.

An effect on people who do not take part in the decision-making is an externality. The person who pays the costs is not always the person who gains.

Ask the class to name the other people in the case: buyers, people who eat the eggs, neighbouring farms. Ask who should pay for a measure whose benefit goes mostly to other people.

A new case: a dairy cooperative

A cooperative of dairy farmers can pay for one measure this year. The options are implementing milking hygiene to lower mastitis, or the testing and removal of cattle with brucellosis. Each option costs the whole budget. The estimated value for the farmers is IDR 80 million for milking hygiene and IDR 60 million for brucellosis control.

The cooperative chooses brucellosis control. What is the opportunity cost?

Key points

  • Veterinary economics is the use of economic principles to decide how to use scarce resources to protect animal health and welfare.
  • Resources are scarce, so every choice gives up something. The opportunity cost of a choice is the value of the best alternative that the choice gives up. It differs from the cost that the owner pays.
  • Disease takes away production, product quality, trade, animal welfare, and public health. Control protects these values.
  • A benefit is private when it goes to the owner who pays, and public when it goes to other people. The person who pays is not always the person who gains.
  • Epidemiology estimates what happens to disease. Economics estimates the value of that change and its costs. The decision maker needs both.
