C1 Epidemiological and economic thinking

Identify levels of analysis

One disease on a farm can be studied in one hen, in the whole flock, or in the eggs that leave the farm. What would you learn from each view?

  • S1.5Identify the levels of analysis in a problem
  • TimeAbout 18 minutes
  • BeforeExplain veterinary economics
Read the guiding question aloud. Ask for a first answer in one sentence. Each view is a level of analysis, a term that slide 4 introduces.

The case: Salmonella on a layer farm

In one week, 120 of the 8,000 hens on a layer farm die. Most of the dead hens come from pens A1 and B1, next to the water inlet.

The laboratory tests five hens and finds Salmonella in all five. Eggs from pens A1 and B1 have already reached two markets.

You are the regulatory assessor at the local animal health authority. You must advise whether the eggs from these pens can stay on sale.

Read the three paragraphs aloud. The farm owner has IDR 15,000,000 left this month (deck 3). The assessor advises the authority, so this deck looks at the same case from a different seat.

Five tested hens are positive for Salmonella. What can the assessor say about the eggs in the two markets?

Choose your prediction.

Let students choose, then ask a student to explain the choice. The answer is C. The next slides explain why: the result describes five hens, and the question is about eggs.

A level of analysis tells what a statement is about

A view of a problem is called a level of analysis. To find the level, ask: what is the statement about?

Statement about the caseWhat it is about
The laboratory finds Salmonella in hen number 3.One hen
120 of the 8,000 hens die in one week.A group of hens
Eggs from pens A1 and B1 reached two markets.The eggs and where they went
Link back to slide 1: each view is a level. Read the three statements. Ask what each one is about before you read the right column. The next two slides give the names of the levels.

Level 1 is one animal. Level 2 is a group of animals.

  • Individual animal: one hen, for example hen number3.
  • Animal population: a group of hens. A pen, a farm, and an area are each a population.

A population is relative. The farm is a population of hens, and it is also one member of the population of farms in the area.

Point to hen number 3, then to the pen, the farm, and the area. Ask: is the farm a population? Students should say yes for the hens, and also see the farm as one unit among the farms of the area.

Level 3 is the chain of animal products and the system around it

The animal product chain and system is the path of animals, feed, and products from the farm to the buyers, with the rules and services around it. The assessor works at this level when the question is where the eggs went.

Trace the eggs from the layer farm to the buyers with a finger. Ask: where would the assessor find records of this path? Open the details to see the three roles of this level.

Every level raises a health question and an economic question

LevelHealth questionEconomic question
Individual animalWhat is wrong with this hen?What is the value of this hen, and what is the cost of its treatment?
Animal populationHow many hens are affected, in which pens, and on which days?What is the owner’s total loss, and how much do the control measures cost?
Animal product chain and systemWhere and when did the eggs go, and who may be exposed?Who else pays or loses: buyers, traders, the egg price?

Economic aspects are present at all three levels. The economic question changes with the level.

Ask students to find the animal, place, and time in the middle health question. Ask which economic question the owner asked in the previous deck. The answer is the middle row.

Each level has its own decision and decision maker

  • Individual animal: the veterinarian and the owner decide whether to treat hen number 3.
  • Animal population: the owner decides whether to repair the water line and disinfect pens A1 and B1.
  • Animal product chain and system: the authority decides whether to withdraw the eggs from the two markets.

A decision at one level moves costs and benefits to other levels. Withdrawing the eggs protects the buyers, but causes the owners to lose revenue.

Ask who pays and who gains in the last bullet. This links to private and public benefits in the previous deck. Open the details for what is at stake at each level.

The assessor sees the case at three levels

LevelFinding in the caseSource of the data

Animal product chain and systemEggs from pens A1 and B1 reached two marketsEgg sales records of the farm

Each row answers a different question, based on different data. No row replaces any other row.

Go through the rows from top to bottom. At the last row ask: which level does the assessor need to decide about the eggs? Open the details to see what each row cannot show.

Why do five positive hens not answer the question about the eggs?

  • Data: five hens, at the individual animal level.
  • Question: are the eggs in the two markets contaminated? This is the chain level.
  • Gap: the data and the question sit at different levels, so the data cannot answer the question.
  • Fix: collect data at the level of the question. Test eggs and trace where they went.

A conclusion stays safe at the level of its data.

Return to the first question of the deck and work it in four lines. Ask students to name the level of the data and the level of the question before you show the gap. Open the details for the jump in the other direction, from a group to one animal.

Find the error

The assessor writes a note.

  1. The laboratory finds Salmonella in five hens.
  2. So no other hen in the flock is infected.
  3. The assessor asks for the egg sales records of pens A1 and B1.

Which sentence draws a conclusion that its data cannot support?

For each sentence ask what it is about and what its data describe. Sentence 3 shows the right move: a question about the eggs needs data on the eggs.

The farm owner says: “Repairing the water line and disinfecting pens A1 and B1 costs IDR 15 million.” At which level does this statement sit?

The assessor wants to know whether the eggs in the first market are contaminated. Which data sit at the level of that question?

A new case: a beef chain

A regulatory assessor studies a beef chain with a cattle farm, a slaughterhouse, and a retail market. At the slaughterhouse, 12 of the 40 cattle in one lot show lesions in the muscle.

Which conclusion goes beyond the level of its data?

Key points

  • The level of analysis tells what a statement is about. The course uses three levels: the individual animal, the animal population, and the animal product chain and system.
  • A population is relative. A pen, a farm, and an area are each a population.
  • Every level raises a health question and an economic question. A decision at one level moves costs and benefits to other levels.
  • Keep a conclusion at the level of its data. When a conclusion needs another level, collect data at that level.
