Three levels of analysis

A problem is studied at one level or at several. Each level has its own question, its own data, and its own decision.

LevelWhat is studiedRole in the course
Individual animalClinical disease, infection, welfare state, productivitySource of population patterns
Animal populationHerd, flock, kennel, shelter, village population, wildlife groupMain level of analysis
Animal product and economic systemMilk, meat, eggs, hides, feed, veterinary medicines, veterinary services, markets, regulationsData source, pathway for introduction, place where economic consequences appear

Zoom through the levels of the case

Start at the inner level and move outward. At each level, ask what data the assessor would need and what decision the level supports.

At which level does the statement sit?

Ask: is the statement about one animal, a group of animals, or the products and the system around them?

Ask the class to vote with a show of hands. Feed deliveries are the statement that students most often place at the population level, because feed reaches the animals.

Key points

  • The level of analysis is the level at which a problem is studied. The course uses three: the individual animal, the animal population, and the animal product and economic system.
  • Each level has its own question, data, and decision. The population is the main level of the course.
  • Most problems span several levels, and a decision at one level often needs evidence from the others.
  • A conclusion at one level does not automatically hold at another, so state the level of every conclusion.
