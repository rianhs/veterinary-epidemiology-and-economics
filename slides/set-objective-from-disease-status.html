Set an objective from the disease status of a population

What should a veterinarian do when a disease is present in a population, and when that disease is absent?

  • TimeAbout 20 minutes
  • BeforeExplain epidemiology
Read the guiding question aloud. Ask for a first answer in one sentence. Students return to this answer at the end of the deck.

The scenario: Salmonella on a layer farm

In one week, 120 of the 8,000 hens on a layer farm die. Most of the dead hens come from pens A1 and B1, which are located near the water inlet.

The laboratory later finds Salmonella in the dead hens. Eggs from these pens have already reached buyers.

You are the program coordinator at the animal health office in this regency.

Ask what the coordinator should work toward on the farm. Keep the answers for the next slide.

Salmonella is now on the farm. Which objective is appropriate for the farm?

Choose your prediction.

Let students choose, then ask a student to explain the choice. Options A and C are the two usual mistakes: they treat a farm with the disease like a farm without it.

Disease status

The disease status within a population is the state of that disease in a population at a specific point in time.

Read the definition aloud. The next slides show what a state can be, using boxes of animals.

The disease in a population is absent, present, or unknown

Each box represents a population, and each circle represents an animal.

In the left box, there are no cases. In the middle box, some animals are cases. In the right box, there is no evidence, so nobody knows which animals are cases.

Point to the three kinds of circle in the key. Ask which box looks like the farm on the Scenario slide. Ask students why the circles in the right box are grey.

Objective for a population

The objective for a population depends on its disease status. The figure shows the same three boxes, with an objective under each box.

When the disease is absent, the objective is to prevent cases. When it is present, the objective is to reduce cases. When the status is unknown, the first objective is to find out the status.

Ask students to say each objective aloud in their own words. Return to the prediction on slide 3 and ask which box the farm belongs to.

Disease may be present at the expected level or above it

The "disease present" group can be further classified based on the number of cases. Cases may occur at an expected level or exceed the expected level.

When a disease is absent, the expected level is zero, so one case is already above it.

Ask: which box is the farm in the week with 120 deaths? Why does the expected level matter for the answer? A free farm has an expected level of zero, so any case is above it.

Disease statuses: absent or unknown

The marked boxes are the groups "disease absent" and "status unknown". Each group has one status.

  • Free: The disease is absent, and evidence shows it. The disease has never been found here, or it was here before and is now gone.
  • Unknown: No evidence shows whether the disease is present or absent. For example, nobody tests the animals.
Ask which status needs tests that would find the disease. Free needs them. Unknown means that nobody has them yet. A disease that was removed earlier is free, with a history.

Disease statuses: present at the expected level

The marked box is the group "present at the expected level". This group has two statuses.

  • Sporadic: Single cases or small groups of cases occur irregularly.
  • Endemic: The disease is always present at the expected level.
Both statuses describe a pattern of occurrence over time. Ask students which one needs several years of data to recognize.

Disease statuses: present above the expected level

The marked box is the group "present above the expected level". This group has three statuses.

  • Outbreak: Cases are above the expected level in a limited place and a limited time.
  • Epidemic: Cases are clearly above the expected level in a wider area or a larger population.
  • Pandemic: An epidemic spreads across several countries or continents.
The three statuses differ in the size of the place and the time. No fixed number of cases separates an outbreak from an epidemic. All three need a comparison with the expected level.

Official status labels

WOAH and the Indonesian government use their own status labels.

WOAH: nine codes for one reporting period

  • Disease present: Cases with clinical signs occur in the whole country.
  • Disease present, limited to zones: Cases with signs occur in some zones.
  • Infection or infestation: Tests confirm the agent, and the animals have no signs.
  • Infection or infestation, limited to zones: The same, in some zones.
  • Disease suspected: A case is suspected in the whole country, and tests have not confirmed it.
  • Disease suspected, limited to zones: The same, in some zones.
  • Disease absent: The disease was absent during the reporting period.
  • Never reported: The disease has never been reported, and is considered historically absent.
  • No information: No information is available on presence or absence.

The Indonesian government: four statuses for areas

  • Free (bebas): The agent has never been found, or it was found earlier and is gone.
  • Suspected (terduga): A free area next to an infected or outbreak area, or an area where the status cannot be set.
  • Infected (tertular): Cases are found in susceptible animals.
  • Outbreak (wabah): An unusual disease event, such as a new disease or a sudden rise in cases.
Students will meet these labels in reports and official maps. WOAH codes describe a country in one semester. Indonesian statuses describe an area. Ask which label in each list is closest to free, and which is closest to unknown.

A new scenario: an animal shelter

A shelter has had no case of canine distemper for two years. In four days, 6 of its 50 dogs show signs of the disease, and 3 test positive.

Which status and objective fit best?

Key points

  • The disease status within a population is the state of that disease in a population at a specific point in time.
  • A population has one of seven statuses. The disease is free or unknown, present at the expected level (sporadic or endemic), or present above it (outbreak, epidemic, or pandemic).
  • When the disease is absent, the objective is to prevent any case. When it is present, the objective is to reduce the cases and, where possible, to eliminate the disease. When the status is unknown, the first objective is to find out the status.
  • A lack of reports does not show that a disease is absent. Free status needs evidence from tests. Without evidence, the status is unknown.
  • WOAH and the Indonesian government use their own status labels in reports and decisions.
