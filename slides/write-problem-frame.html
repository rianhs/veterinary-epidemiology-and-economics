Write a problem frame

What must a short statement of a population-level health problem contain before anyone can look for information?

The case: Salmonella on a layer farm

A layer farm with 8,000 hens in 8 pens lost 120 hens in one week, mostly in pens A1 and B1. The laboratory found Salmonella in the five hens that it received, and the farm had no earlier positive result.

Your role: technical service consultant (GP3.1), two years after graduation. The farm owner asks you what to do and wants information about Salmonella.

These numbers are synthetic and are for teaching.

Read the two sentences aloud. Ask the class what the owner really needs, and what a consultant would do with a request for everything.

A problem frame has five parts

A problem frame is a short written statement of a population-level health problem. Each part answers one question and builds on an earlier skill.

PartQuestionBuilt on
PopulationWhich group of animals?S1.6, levels of analysis
OutcomeWhich health event or consequence matters?S1.1, the definition of epidemiology
DecisionWhich choice needs information?S1.7, the five questions
Decision makerWho chooses?S1.2, choices in veterinary economics
ObjectiveReduce the problem or keep it out?S1.5, population status

Build the frame for the owner of the farm

Choose one option for each part in the controls. The slide tells you whether each part fits.

Let students choose in pairs. The wrong options are mistakes that students often make. They swap a sample for a population, a measure for an outcome, and the adviser for the decision maker. They also put a question for information in place of a decision.

Which part of the frame is missing?

Check the five parts one by one: population, outcome, decision, decision maker, and objective.

Each frame lacks exactly one part. Ask students to say which question the missing part would answer, and which information the reader still cannot look for.

The decision maker changes the frame

The case stays the same. Choose a decision maker in the controls and read the frame.

Select a decision maker in the controls.

Ask which parts changed. The population, the decision, and the objective all change when the person who chooses changes. This is the reason that a frame names the decision maker.

The laboratory reports Salmonella in the hens. The owner asks the consultant what to do. Who is the decision maker in the frame?

A new case: a fish farm

A technical service consultant (GP3.1) advises a fish farm with 20 ponds. Fish die in 3 ponds, and the cause is not known yet. The farm manager must decide whether to treat the 3 ponds or all 20. This scenario is synthetic and is for teaching.

Which statement is the outcome of the problem frame?

Key points

  • A problem frame states a population-level health problem in at most three sentences, with five parts: population, outcome, decision, decision maker, and objective.
  • The decision turns a request for everything into a question that information can answer.
  • The same case gives a different frame for a different decision maker.
  • A missing part shows which information the reader cannot look for yet.
