Match population status to objective

What is the state of a disease in a population, and which objective does that state point to?

The case: Salmonella in the egg chain of a district

A district in South Sulawesi has 60 layer farms that sell eggs to local markets. One farm lost 120 hens in a week, and the laboratory found Salmonella in the hens and in eggs that had already reached buyers.

Your role: program coordinator at the district livestock service (GP4.1), two years after graduation. The head of the service asks what the district should aim for.

The status points to one of two objectives

If the disease is present in the population, the objective is to reduce the problem. If the disease is absent, the objective is to keep it out.

Walk along the range of statuses

Move from left to right. At each status, ask which of the two objectives applies. Point out the three groups in the table on the previous slide.

Which objective does the situation call for?

Ask: is the disease present in the population now?

Ask the class to vote with a show of hands. The exposed status and the under-control status are the two that students most often place under the wrong objective.

A new case: an animal shelter

A shelter has had no case of canine distemper for two years. Within four days, 6 of its 50 dogs show signs and 3 test positive. This scenario is synthetic and is for teaching.

Which status and objective fit best?

Key points

  • The population status is the state of a disease in a defined population at a given time. It runs from free to eliminated.
  • If the disease is present, the objective is to reduce the problem. If the disease is absent, the objective is to keep it out.
  • The status can change, and the objective changes with it. An outbreak in a free population moves the aim from keeping out to containing.
  • A claim of freedom needs evidence. Silence from a population with no surveillance leaves the status unknown.
