Match the disease status of a population to an objective

What should a veterinarian do when a disease is present in a population, and when that disease is absent?

  • TimeAbout 15 minutes
  • BeforeExplain epidemiology
Read the guiding question aloud. Ask for a first answer in one sentence. Students return to this answer at the end of the deck.

The case: Salmonella on a layer farm

In one week, 120 of the 8,000 hens on a layer farm die. Most of the dead hens come from pens A1 and B1, which are located near the water inlet.

The laboratory later finds Salmonella in the hens. Eggs from these pens have already reached buyers.

You are the program coordinator at the animal health office in this regency.

Ask what the coordinator should work toward on the farm. Keep the answers for the next slide.

Salmonella is now on the farm. Which objective is appropriate for the farm?

Choose your prediction.

Let students choose, then ask a student to explain the choice. Options A and C are the two usual mistakes: they treat a farm with the disease like a farm without it.

Disease status

The disease status within a population is the state of that disease in a population at a specific point in time.

Read the definition aloud. The next slides show what a state can be, using boxes of animals.

A disease is absent or present in a population

Each box represents a population, and each circle represents an animal.
In the left box, there are no cases. In the right box, some animals are cases.

Point to the two kinds of circle in the key. Ask which box looks like the farm on the case slide.

The objective depends on whether the disease is there

The objective for a population depends on its disease status. There are two types of objectives: preventing cases and reducing cases.

Ask students to say each objective aloud in their own words. Return to the prediction on slide 3 and ask which box the farm belongs to.

Disease may be present at the expected level or above it

The "disease present" group can be further classified based on the number of cases. Cases may occur at an expected level or exceed the expected level.

Ask: which box is the farm in the week with 120 deaths? Why does the expected level matter for the answer?

Disease statuses: absent or not established

  • Free: The disease is absent, and evidence shows it.
  • Exposed, disease not established: The agent has entered the population, but the disease is not yet present.
  • Eliminated: The disease is removed from a defined area. Interventions are implemented to prevent its return.
Ask which of the three statuses needs the most evidence. Free and eliminated both need tests that would find the disease.

Disease statuses: present at an expected or low level

  • Sporadic: Single cases or small groups of cases occur irregularly.
  • Endemic: The disease is always present at the expected level.
  • Under control: Interventions keep the cases at a low level. The work goes on until the disease is eliminated.
Sporadic and endemic describe a pattern of occurrence. Under control describes the result of interventions.

Disease statuses: present above the expected level

  • Outbreak: Cases are above the expected level in a limited place and a limited time.
  • Epidemic: Cases are clearly above the expected level in a wider area or a larger population.
  • Pandemic: An epidemic spreads across several countries or continents.
The three statuses differ in the size of the place and the time. All three need a comparison with the expected level.

Complete each statement

1. Laboratory tests find Salmonella in chicks that arrive at one farm in Regency G. The farm owner removes all the chicks at once, and no hen gets the disease.

The status is exposed, disease not established. The objective is .

2. The number of Salmonella cases in Regency H stays low because of a control program. Cases still occur.

The status is . The objective is .

3. Veterinarians removed Salmonella from all layer farms of Regency K three years ago. Tests continue, and all results are negative.

The status is . The objective is .

In statement 1 the status is given, so students only choose the objective. In statements 2 and 3 they choose both. Statement 2 is the usual mistake: the disease is present, so the objective is to reduce the cases.

Find the error

A student writes a note about Regency C.

"No farm of Regency C has reported Salmonella in five years. The status of Regency C is free."

What does the student need before writing that the status is free?

The note takes a lack of reports as evidence. Ask students who would report if no one tests.

Salmonella spreads from the farm. Deaths are far above the expected number on 12 farms in many regencies and provinces. Which status fits?

Regency C is free of Salmonella, and test results show it. A truck brings chicks from Regency A, where Salmonella is endemic. Which objective fits Regency C?

A new case: an animal shelter

A shelter has had no case of canine distemper for two years. In four days, 6 of its 50 dogs show signs of the disease, and 3 test positive.

Which status and objective fit best?

Key points

  • The disease status within a population is the state of that disease in a population at a specific point in time.
  • When the disease is absent, the objective is to prevent any case. When it is present, the objective is to reduce the cases and, where possible, to eliminate the disease.
  • A disease that is present is at an expected or low level, or above the expected level. The nine statuses give each group more exact names.
  • A lack of reports does not show that a disease is absent. Free status needs evidence from tests.
