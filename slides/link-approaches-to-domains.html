The case: a layer farm in South Sulawesi

The farm loses 120 of its 8,000 hens in one week, mostly in two pens next to the water inlet. The laboratory later finds Salmonella in the hens, and eggs from the same pens have already reached buyers.

Your role: veterinary laboratory diagnostician (GP2.1), two years after graduation. The farm sends you five hens and asks what the result means.

Each question has a domain and an approach

Domain 0 comes first and asks why epidemiology is needed. The five questions follow in this order.

DomainQuestionApproachElement of the definition
D1What is happening?Descriptive epidemiologyDistribution
D2Why is it happening?Analytical epidemiology and experimental epidemiologyDeterminants
D3What could happen?Predictive epidemiologyProjection of what could happen
D4What should be done?Interventional epidemiologyApplication: prevention and control
D5Did it work?EvaluationApplication: checking the results

Which question does the statement answer?

Ask: does the statement describe, explain, project, choose, or check?

Ask the class to vote with a show of hands before you reveal each answer. The statement about the owner choosing between culling, vaccination, and egg testing separates the work of the laboratory from the decision of the owner.

The loop turns observation into action

Move step by step through the case. At step 4 ask why two domains share this step. At step 8 show that the loop starts again with new information.

After the owner repairs the water line, the laboratory tests the flock again. Which step of the loop is this?

A new case: dead waterbirds at a lake

A wildlife veterinarian (GP1.3) finds 30 dead waterbirds at a lake in one week. She counts the dead birds by day and by site on the shore. This scenario is synthetic and is for teaching.

Which approach does this counting represent?

Key points

  • Each element of the definition of epidemiology leads to an approach, and each approach answers one of five questions.
  • The five questions are: what is happening, why is it happening, what could happen, what should be done, and did it work.
  • The information-to-action loop turns observation of a population into action, and the action changes the population that is observed next.
  • A problem can be placed in the five questions by asking which step of the loop it belongs to.
